# Vyrnok

> A green candle burns inside the tooth of a creature larger than the dawn.

## The Candle Seller's Inventory

The candle seller keeps her wares in a chest lined with white stone. Each candle has a different scent when unlit: wet wool, old apples, a room recently swept. Once lit, they all smell of an unfamiliar shore.

One green candle rests inside a curved tooth. It has been burning since the chest was found. Its wax runs upward into the root, where it gathers around a small dark opening.

The seller counts her candles every evening. The number is always correct, but the green flame appears in a different place in her account. Sometimes it is listed between two white candles. Sometimes it occupies the space where she intended to write her own name.

She has stopped signing the inventory. The chest has grown quieter since then.

## The Customer Who Brought a Shadow

The customer arrived at midday with a second shadow folded beneath his coat. His ordinary shadow followed him into the shop. The folded one remained against his chest, making the cloth move as if a small bird were sleeping there.

He asked for a candle that would illuminate only what had been hidden deliberately. The seller placed a plain taper on the counter. Its flame showed the shop, the street, and the dark space beneath his coat with equal care.

He shook his head. The folded shadow stirred. Inside the stone-lined chest, the green candle leaned toward him.

The seller asked whose shadow he was carrying. He said it had belonged to a door before the door was removed from his childhood house. Beyond that door lay a room everyone remembered differently.

## A Room Divided by Accounts

His mother remembered a pantry. His father remembered a staircase. His sister remembered a bed beneath a window, though the house had no window on that side.

The customer remembered nothing beyond the threshold. He remembered only a hand on his shoulder and someone telling him that the room was not yet ready to be seen.

Years later, he had found the removed door in a shed. Its shadow lay folded against the wall, untouched by the afternoon sun. When he lifted it, the wood became ordinary firewood, and the space behind it filled with the smell of an unfamiliar shore.

The seller opened her chest. The green flame cast no light on the tooth. It illuminated the folded shadow through the customer's coat, revealing a narrow staircase inside it.

## The Tooth's Former Place

The tooth had belonged to a dragon whose jaw formed the cliffs of that shore. Its breath moved the tides. Its tongue held a town built from rooms that had been withheld from sight.

Every room had a keeper. Every keeper carried a candle. When a room was ready, its keeper crossed the town and placed the candle inside the tooth nearest the sea.

The seller had discovered the chest after a storm left it outside her shop. Inside were candles, white stone, and an inventory written in her own hand. She had not recognized the shore until the first customer lit a taper and described the same salt wind she had dreamed since childhood.

The green candle had never been sold. No customer had previously brought a room's shadow folded close enough to keep it warm.

## Four Cuts Beneath the Flame

The tooth carried four cuts along its inner curve:

*Vyr — nok — ul — sheth.*

The customer pronounced them while unfolding the shadow. It spread across the counter and became an open doorway. Beyond it lay a pantry, a staircase, and a bed, occupying the same small room without touching.

On the bed rested a hand that appeared to be waiting for a shoulder. Beside the stairs stood a candle seller younger than the woman behind the counter. In the pantry, someone closed a jar containing a little green light.

The flame within the tooth grew straight. The customer stepped close enough to see the room's missing window reflected in its wax. Outside that window, an enormous eye watched the shoreline through a curtain of rain.

He placed his ordinary shadow across the threshold. The folded shadow rose to meet it, and both became briefly transparent.

## An Item Removed Without Sale

The doorway closed. The customer stood beside the counter with his coat open and one shadow beneath his feet. The green candle had gone out, leaving a little pool of clear wax inside the tooth.

The seller wrote the day's inventory. For the first time, she found a line already crossed out. Beside it was a drawing of a window facing the sea.

She gave the customer the tooth wrapped in plain paper. He carried it away without asking whether the candle could be relit. At the corner, his shadow paused while he continued walking, then caught up before he reached the next doorway.

The chest remains lined with white stone. Its candles still smell of an unfamiliar shore.

But somewhere beyond that shore, a room has acquired a window, and a creature larger than the dawn holds its mouth closed against a light it no longer needs to hide.
